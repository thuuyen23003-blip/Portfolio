Digitalizing The Pizza Company

Peer-led group (no team leader) · Management Information Systems Course

An analysis of current operations and a proposed digitalization concept for a restaurant chain, focused on the reservation, ordering, and real-time kitchen coordination experience.

My Contribution

  • Originated the concept for three subsystems: floor-plan-based table reservation, order routing with kitchen synchronization, and real-time preparation tracking. The remaining subsystems (QR ordering, CRM, order confirmation, and feedback handling) were developed collaboratively as a team.
  • Did not build the interfaces directly, but served as the main advisor shaping the design decisions behind the final product.
  • Reviewed content and slides prepared by teammates. As the team worked without a designated leader, my role leaned toward shaping ideas and providing critical feedback.

Key Design Decisions

  • Modeled table selection as a visual floor plan — similar to choosing a seat for a flight or a movie — rather than a simple "available / not available" indicator, so customers could compare zones and table sizes before arriving.
  • Designed a fallback for no-shows: a reservation holds the table for a fixed window (e.g. 15 minutes), after which it is automatically released, with a reminder sent to the customer beforehand.
  • Kept a manual ordering path alongside QR ordering for customers less comfortable with the technology, or in case of a network outage — the system was designed to support staff, not replace them.
  • Allowed a short edit window (3 minutes) after an order is placed, before it locks and is sent to the kitchen, balancing flexibility for customers with stability for kitchen operations.
  • When asked to prioritize a single subsystem for a limited budget, chose order routing & kitchen synchronization first, since it sits at the center of the workflow — connecting customers, service staff, and the kitchen — ahead of subsystems like CRM or feedback collection.

Considered and set aside: using AI cameras or sensors to automatically detect when a table becomes occupied or vacated. The idea was shelved due to hardware cost and implementation complexity, and flagged as a possible future direction rather than part of this project's scope.

1. Table Reservation by Floor Plan

Customers enter their party size and preferred time, then pick an available table directly from a visual floor map instead of an anonymous table number, giving staff an accurate, real-time view of seating status.

2. In-Table Online Ordering

Each table has a unique QR code; scanning it opens the menu already linked to that table, so orders are placed and billed correctly without a server needing to take them down manually.

3. Order Routing & Kitchen Synchronization

Once placed, an order is automatically routed to the kitchen as a preparation ticket; as items are finished, they move to a "ready to serve" list that floor staff can act on immediately, removing the need for verbal handoffs between kitchen and service staff.

4. Real-Time Preparation Tracking

Both staff and customers can see live status per order: the kitchen dashboard shows what's cooking and what's done, the service dashboard shows what's ready to deliver, and customers see their own order's progress from their phone.
